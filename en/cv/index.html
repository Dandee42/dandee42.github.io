My story

I didn’t get into development through a computer science degree, but by a detour through e-commerce and a lot of self-study. I’m more of a generalist than a specialist. I like to understand a product as a whole, from the e-shop and marketing through development to testing. And I enjoy learning new things.

The e-shop and my first automations

From 2018 I worked as the administrator of a mid-sized e-shop. I was also in charge of email marketing, and email campaigns were where I first got into automation.

While the e-shop was being developed, I was also lucky enough to look over the developers’ shoulders. It opened up a world I hadn’t known before. I enjoyed taking part in the development step by step: coming up with new features, testing them and looking for solutions to the problems at hand.

New Zealand

Between 2022 and 2024 I spent 15 months in New Zealand on a Working Holiday. I practised my English and made a lot of friends from abroad.

Coming back and getting into IT

After coming back from New Zealand in 2024, I decided to make a major career change and threw myself into learning. I learned the basics of programming and completed several courses focused on web application development and testing in Python. I learned to work with Git and to build simple web applications in Django, such as my fitness app BodyLog.

My first IT job

My first job in IT was as a manual tester. It soon grew to include automated tests in Playwright, and the role became more and more like a QA engineer’s.

Alongside that, I started playing with n8n and built both simple and more complex automations at the company. I loved it, and I kept coming up with ideas for what to automate next.

I used to design and build everything by hand; today I get help from AI. That manual work was important for understanding the technical foundations, so when AI suggests something, I understand what it’s doing.

Where I am today

I have built several websites and other web applications that, unfortunately, I can’t show publicly.

My experience in e-commerce and online marketing helps me look at every project through a business lens: what the product should achieve and for whom. Today I build web applications in Django and Next.js, AI solutions on top of company data and automations in n8n, from the first idea through a prototype to launch. I work primarily with Claude Code, which gets me to a working product faster, and Playwright tests make sure nothing breaks along the way. I keep security, scalability and maintainability in mind, so the product holds up as it grows.

My goal is to build products that not only save time, but also make sense.
